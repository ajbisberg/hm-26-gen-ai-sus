# Discussion Role Cards

Print single-sided and cut along the rules. Tuesday uses the five **analytic-team** roles (×2 teams); Wednesday uses the five **fishbowl observer** roles (one set; roles swap with the circles at halftime).

---

## Tuesday — Analytic-team roles

> **PROPONENT** — Build the strongest case *for* the statement, using evidence from the paper. Numbers beat adjectives. Any teammate may be asked to defend your points — brief them.

---

> **CRITIC** — Find where the statement overreaches. What are the limits of the paper's data? What claim goes beyond what was measured?

---

> **ILLUSTRATOR** — Choose the 2–3 most persuasive numbers or equivalencies in the paper and be ready to explain each in one plain sentence a non-expert would remember.

---

> **SKEPTIC** — Ask the hard methods questions: what couldn't the authors measure? How far do findings from these models generalize? Would the result survive at a different scale?

---

> **SUMMARIZER** — Track the team's argument as it forms. You deliver the 5-minute verdict — but anyone may be called on, so keep the whole team synced.

---

## Wednesday — Fishbowl observer roles

> **EVIDENCE TRACKER** — Note which claims are backed by numbers from the paper and which are vibes. Report the best-evidenced and least-evidenced claim you heard.

---

> **CONNECTOR** — Listen for links to Tuesday's paper and to course concepts (routing, loops, energy labels, minimization). Report the strongest connection made — and one nobody made.

---

> **DEVIL'S ADVOCATE** — Identify the strongest point *nobody* in the circle made. You get 30 seconds in the report-out to make it.

---

> **BALANCE MONITOR** — Track who spoke, who was cut off, and which moves invited others in. Report one pattern, without naming and shaming.

---

> **SUMMARIZER** — Capture the 2–3 ideas the circle converged on. You open the report-out.
